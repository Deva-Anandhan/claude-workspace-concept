What if your ambition wasn't limited by the team you can afford?

Why I made this

It started with a question I couldn't shake

I'm a GTM Engineer, and I use Claude almost every day to build automations, connect systems and try ideas I wouldn't have attempted alone.

Over time I noticed something. If Claude is where I think, plan and build, why does finishing the work still depend on a dozen other apps, each with its own logins, keys and rules?

The idea

Ask. Approve. Done.

Agents get what they need to finish a job, and people keep the decisions that matter.

Ask

In chat or mail: fix this contract, renew that tool, set up the new hire.

Approve

An agent does the work and stops at the limits you set: a discount, a purchase, a release.

Done

It finishes, reports back, and every step is on the record.

How it fits together

Three layers, and control matters most

Claude decides what should happen. A separate control layer decides what it's allowed to do.

IntelligenceWhat should happen?Understanding intent, planning, memory, coordinating agents
ControlWhat is it allowed to do?Identity, permissions, credentials, spending limits, approvals, audit
ExecutionHow does it get done?APIs, MCP, browser, mail, files, code, deployment

Open questions

What I don't know yet

These are the questions I find most interesting, and I'd value other views on them.

  • How should agents authenticate to apps designed for humans?
  • How should credentials be brokered across different vendors?
  • How reliable can browser execution get where no API exists?
  • Who is responsible when an agent spends money by mistake?
  • How does a person stay meaningfully in control as agents get faster?

Maybe the future of work isn't more applications. It's more people able to build something real.
