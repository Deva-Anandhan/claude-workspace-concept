An independent concept

What if a company ran by asking?

AI can already write the software. What it still cannot do alone is sign in, hold a key, read the mail, or pay. This concept imagines Claude with all of those built in, so a person asks, approves, and the work gets done.

The observation

Building got cheap. Everything around it did not.

A CRM, an automation or an internal tool that once needed a team and a stack of paid software can now be written by an AI in hours. The scarce thing is no longer the code.

But today every AI agent is a guest in someone else's house. It browses in another company's browser, signs in through another company's login, reads work in another company's mail and chat, and cannot buy anything. At each of those points a person has to step in: buy the subscription, create the account, paste a token, pass along the message.

The person is the glue. This concept removes the glue.

The pieces

Six things an agent needs to finish a job

Claude Browser

A full browser where bots work in their own tabs, as themselves. You can watch, and take over at any moment.

Claude Auth

An identity for every bot, with a role and scoped access, so apps are authorised directly.

Claude Vault

Credentials a bot can use but never read. No more pasting tokens into a chat.

Claude Mail and Chat

Where work arrives and gets reported. Bots sort the inbox, draft replies and pick up requests.

Claude Pay

A funded wallet with budgets and limits. A bot quotes, you approve, and it pays inside the rules.

Claude Bots

Persistent teammates with a role, an owner and a budget, working side by side all day.

Underneath all six sits one more layer: consent and policy. It records what each bot may access, spend and send, who approved it and when. Money moves only inside an approval a person gave.

How it works

Ask. Approve. Done.

  1. Someone asks in chat or mail: fix this contract, renew that subscription, set up the new hire.
  2. A bot picks it up, signs in as itself, and uses keys it never sees.
  3. It does the work in its own browser session, in the tools the company already has.
  4. When it reaches a limit you set, such as a discount, a purchase or a release, it stops and asks.
  5. You approve with one tap. It finishes, pays if needed, and reports back.
  6. Every step is on the record.

For an individual, this is the bigger change. A good idea used to need money to hire the people who would execute it. If execution costs a subscription and a wallet budget, the filter on who gets to build shifts from who has capital to who has ideas and judgment.

The contest

This is about the layer where work happens

Each era of computing was won by whoever owned the default place where work gets done. Today that is the browser, mail, identity and documents, built for a person looking at a screen. When an agent does the work, nobody opens those screens, and whoever the agent belongs to becomes the interface.

What has to be solved

The hard parts are trust, not software

An agent that holds keys and money must not be tricked by what it reads, and someone has to be clearly responsible when it spends wrongly. That is why every sensitive step in this concept runs through a limit and an approval, why secrets are never shown to the model, and why everything is logged. Whoever earns that trust earns the layer.
