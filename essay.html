What if Claude could change the way we work?

It started with using Claude every day. It turned into a concept for a different way of working.

DA
Deva Anandhan LinkedIn ↗
GTM Engineer, systems and automation · November 2026 · 5 min read

I'm a GTM Engineer. Most of my time goes into building AI employees, systems and automations at scale, and connecting them so work flows between them. Claude is part of nearly all of it: code, integrations, research, workflows, and ideas I wouldn't have attempted on my own.

Often a simple conversation turns into something I can actually build. Somewhere along the way I realised I wasn't just using Claude to work faster. It was changing how I approach work. Which left me with a question I couldn't shake:

If Claude is where I think, plan and build, why does finishing the work still depend on a dozen other applications?

We made AI capable. We haven't rebuilt work around it.

Even when Claude understands exactly what I want, getting it done means logins, permissions, API keys, and copying things from one place to another. A workflow that looks simple on paper gets complicated because every app has its own rules.

To be clear, I'm not suggesting Anthropic rebuild Gmail, Salesforce or GitHub. Those products, their APIs and MCP all stay. The difference I care about is between an assistant that connects to many apps and a workspace that manages the whole life of a piece of work across them.

Six walls I keep running into

Building systems and automations every day, I hit the same problems. Each one is a design question for a workspace like this.

What one person could build

This is the part that excites me most. Imagine a founder with an idea and no team, opening Claude and typing:

You aren't moving between apps. You're directing the work.

This doesn't make people unnecessary. Judgment, relationships and taste still matter most. But the distance between having an idea and building it could shrink dramatically, and that matters most for the people who can't afford a team today: freelancers, creators, small businesses and first-time founders.

What if the size of your ambition was no longer limited by the size of the team you can afford?

Three layers, and why control matters most

Claude should understand what needs to happen. The control layer should decide what it's actually allowed to do. Keeping those two separate is, to me, the most important part of the whole idea. A workspace that can act on your behalf has to be one you can trust: limits you set, approvals you give, and a record of everything.

What I built

I didn't want this to stay a note on my laptop, so I built a front-end concept: Chat, Mail, Bots, Browser and Settings, plus a two-minute film. It's an independent project and the interactions are scripted, but building it moved me from imagining the experience to thinking about how it would be structured.

Why this connects to Anthropic

I believe technology should expand what people can do, not just make old processes slightly faster. Anthropic's aim of AI that is capable, reliable, interpretable and steerable is the bar a workspace like this would have to meet. Something that can sign in to your systems and spend your money needs safety and human oversight as requirements, not afterthoughts.

I don't want to watch this shift happen. I want to help build it.

Maybe the future of work isn't more applications. Maybe it's more people able to build something real with what they already have.
